Sarah runs a café in West Jerusalem and is married to an Israeli army officer. Saleem delivers bread to her café from the east of the city, and his wife is expecting their first child. They meet in his van at night, in the parking lots and back streets where nobody looks twice. Then one evening they drive across to Bethlehem, something goes wrong in a bar, and a lie told to cover it starts to travel.

What starts as a domestic drama becomes a surveillance thriller. In Jerusalem, nothing personal stays private for long. An affair that would be a scandal anywhere else turns into a security matter, a file, a case for interrogators on both sides of the wall. Muayad Alayan, working from a script by his brother Rami, is less interested in the romance than in the machinery that grinds through it. The two lovers are punished very differently, and the film is clear-eyed about why.

The best thing here is the two wives. They start as background figures and end up carrying the film's moral weight, two women paying for choices they never made. Alayan shoots on real streets with a handheld, unshowy camera, and the tension never relies on twists. It comes from knowing exactly how unequal the ground is. A small Palestinian film with a very large reach: an infidelity story that turns out to be about everything else.
