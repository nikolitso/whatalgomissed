Omar is a young Palestinian baker in the West Bank who routinely climbs the separation wall, under fire if necessary, to visit Nadia, the sister of his childhood friend Tarek. He is shy with her and fearless everywhere else. With Tarek and their friend Amjad he plans an attack on an Israeli army post. After a soldier is killed, Omar is arrested, beaten and tricked into a confession, and then given a choice by an Israeli agent: prison, or work as an informant.

Hany Abu-Assad turns this into a taut thriller about loyalty, betrayal and what occupation does to trust between people who should be on the same side. The wall is everywhere, and the film's physical energy comes from it: chases through alleys, climbs, falls. Adam Bakri, in his first lead role, is excellent as Omar, and Leem Lubany gives Nadia a quiet strength. Waleed Zuaiter makes the handler Rami almost likeable, which is the most disturbing thing about him.

The film is less interested in heroes than in the cracks the system creates between friends and lovers. Once Omar is compromised, nobody can be sure of anybody, and every conversation becomes a test. It won the Jury Prize in Un Certain Regard at Cannes and was nominated for the Oscar, Abu-Assad's second nomination after Paradise Now. Lean, intelligent and heartbreaking.
