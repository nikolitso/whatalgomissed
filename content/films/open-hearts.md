Cecilie and Joachim are young, happy and about to be married. Then, outside a restaurant, Joachim is hit by a car and left paralysed from the neck down. The driver was Marie, distracted by an argument with her teenage daughter. Her husband Niels is a doctor at the hospital where Joachim is treated. Out of guilt, Marie asks Niels to look after Cecilie. He does. And Cecilie, pushed away by a furious, bitter Joachim, falls in love with him.

This is Susanne Bier's Dogme film, number 28 in the movement, written with Anders Thomas Jensen. Handheld camera, natural light, no score worth the name, and Bier handles the impossible premise with the unflinching precision that would define her career. The infidelity is not glamorised or condemned. It is simply shown as what happens when grief collides with the proximity of someone who is present when you need it most.

The acting is extraordinary across the board. Sonja Richter is raw as Cecilie, Nikolaj Lie Kaas almost unbearable to watch as Joachim, and Paprika Steen gives Marie a dignity that makes the betrayal hurt more. Mads Mikkelsen, in an early role, shows everything he was about to become: a decent man doing something indecent, and knowing it. Bier went on to Brothers, After the Wedding and an Oscar, but this may be her best. Harrowing and humane.
