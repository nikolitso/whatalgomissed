Titta Di Girolamo is a middle-aged Italian man who has lived for eight years in a hotel in the Swiss city of Lugano. He follows a rigid daily routine, speaks to almost no one, plays cards with an aristocratic couple who have lost everything, and injects heroin every Wednesday at the same hour. Once a week, a suitcase full of money arrives. He takes it to the bank. Then he begins to notice the barmaid, Sofia, who has served him for a long time without ever getting a word in return.

This was Paolo Sorrentino's breakthrough, and his first great collaboration with Toni Servillo. It is cool, precise and deeply unsettling beneath its surface stillness. Sorrentino's style is already fully formed: the elegant camera moves, the symmetrical frames, the unexpected music, the deadpan humour. Every shot looks designed, and yet the film is full of feeling. The hotel becomes a kind of prison, and Titta its most polite inmate.

The revelation of why he is there, and what he owes, arrives with the force of a collision. I won't say more. The film competed at Cannes and won five David di Donatello Awards, including Best Film. Toni Servillo, with his frozen face and sudden flashes of irony, gives his finest early performance, the one that led to Il Divo and The Great Beauty. If you only know late Sorrentino, start here. Cold on the surface, burning underneath.
