Ida is a hairdresser in Copenhagen who has just finished chemotherapy. She comes home early to find her husband Leif on the sofa with a young woman from his office. Days later she has to travel alone to Sorrento for her daughter's wedding, and in the airport car park she literally reverses into Philip, a widowed English businessman who lives in Denmark and turns out to be the father of the groom. He is rude, closed and alone. You can guess the rest, and that is fine.

This is Susanne Bier's lightest film by far, a romantic comedy set in the Italian sun, made two years after her Oscar for In a Better World. The Danish title translates as "The Bald Hairdresser", which tells you more about its spirit than the English one. Bier keeps some of her usual edge: illness, betrayal and family lies are all on the table, and the wedding party is a gallery of embarrassing relatives. But the lemon groves and the sea are allowed to do their work.

Pierce Brosnan is relaxed and better than you'd expect. Kim Bodnia is very funny as the useless husband. But the film is carried entirely by Trine Dyrholm, who makes even the lightest material feel inhabited and specific. Her Ida is bruised, practical and much braver than she knows. Not a great film. A warm one, which is sometimes exactly what you need.
