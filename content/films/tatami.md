Iranian judoka Leila and her coach Maryam arrive at the Women's World Judo Championships in Tbilisi with a real chance at gold. Leila wins her first bouts with ease. Then, midway through the tournament, a message arrives from the Islamic Republic: she must fake an injury and withdraw, so that she can never face an Israeli opponent. Refuse, and she will be branded a traitor, and her family back home will pay the price.

Shot in black and white, in a tight, boxy frame, with the urgency of a thriller, it was directed by Zar Amir Ebrahimi, herself an exile from Iran and the Best Actress winner at Cannes for Holy Spider, together with the Israeli filmmaker Guy Nattiv. It is the first Iranian-Israeli cinematic collaboration, made in secret. Ebrahimi also plays Maryam, a former champion who once had to make the same choice, and Arienne Mandi is fierce and physical as Leila. The film cuts between the mat, the warm-up rooms and the phones ringing back home, and the tension never lets up.

Every match Leila wins tightens the noose. The story is fiction, but it is drawn from real cases of Iranian athletes forced to throw contests, and you feel that reality in every scene. The judo is filmed brilliantly, close and sweaty and exhausting. Sport as the purest form of political resistance, where your body is the only argument left. A brave film, and a gripping one.
