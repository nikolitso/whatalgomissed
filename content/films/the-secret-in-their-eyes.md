Benjamín Espósito has retired from the Buenos Aires criminal courts and decides to write a novel about the one case he never got over: the rape and murder of a young woman in 1974. He takes the manuscript to Irene, his former boss, the woman he has loved for twenty-five years without ever saying so. The film moves between the two times, and slowly you realise that the investigation and the unspoken love story are the same story.

Ricardo Darín is perfect as Espósito, tired, ironic and stubborn, and Guillermo Francella, best known in Argentina as a comic actor, is wonderful as his alcoholic colleague Sandoval. Campanella, who adapted Eduardo Sacheri's novel, gives the thriller real momentum. There is a famous sequence in a packed football stadium, a single unbroken shot that swoops from above the pitch into the crowd. It is one of the great showpieces of the decade.

But the film is about more than catching a killer. In the background is the Argentina of the mid-seventies, the years just before the dictatorship, when violent men could find protection in the state and justice was something you might have to arrange yourself. It won the Oscar for Best Foreign Language Film and became one of the biggest hits in Argentine history. A thriller, a love story and a meditation on justice and memory, building to one of the most shocking final scenes in recent cinema.
