A family saga spanning Italy from 1966 to 2003: six hours that feel like a gift, not a commitment. Brothers Nicola and Matteo Carati start out as Roman students with the summer ahead of them. A chance encounter with Giorgia, a young woman held in a psychiatric clinic, changes both their lives, and from there their paths diverge. Nicola becomes a psychiatrist. Matteo, restless and angry, joins the police.

Through them, Marco Tullio Giordana travels through the most turbulent decades of postwar Italian history: the Florence flood, the student movement, the Red Brigades, the reform of mental asylums, the Mafia killings in Sicily. History is always there, but it never crushes the characters; it simply happens to them, the way it happens to all of us. Luigi Lo Cascio and Alessio Boni are magnificent as the brothers, and Sonia Bergamasco, Maya Sansa and Jasmine Trinca give the women of the story real weight. Turin, Rome, Palermo and Florence all become part of the family album.

It was made for Italian television and shown in two parts, but it won the Un Certain Regard Prize at Cannes and found audiences all over the world. Giordana makes you feel the weight of each choice without ever making the film feel heavy. It is about brothers, parents, lovers, children and a whole country trying to grow up. The kind of film you wish would never end. My favourite Italian film.
