In a small Greek town, amorous passion collides with greed, and the bodies start piling up. Lovers, husbands, small-time crooks and people who want a bit more than they have: everyone is scheming, everyone is lying, and nobody is half as clever as they think. It sounds like a crime story, and it is one. But this is Yannis Economides, so it is also something stranger.

This is his most operatic film. The director of Matchbox, Soul Kicking and Knifer has always filmed Greek society at full volume, and here he turns it up further: loud, dark, blackly comic, and completely committed to its own internal logic. The dialogue is a torrent of insults and declarations. Characters fall in love and betray each other in the same breath. The music swells where it shouldn't, and the violence arrives with no warning at all.

It is not for everyone. The tone shifts between farce and tragedy without notice, and the film seems to enjoy the discomfort this causes. Some will find it exhausting. But if you surrender to it, there is something genuinely wild and alive here that Greek cinema rarely permits itself: a filmmaker who doesn't care about good taste, only about truth, even when the truth is grotesque. It became a surprise box office hit in Greece despite, or because of, its refusal to behave. Excessive, uneven, and impossible to ignore.
