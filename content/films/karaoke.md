Tuvia and Yaffa have been married for decades and live a quiet, comfortable life in a high-rise apartment block near Tel Aviv. Their days follow a routine, their conversations have shrunk to the practical, and their children have grown up. Then Itzik, a charismatic, wealthy bachelor with a taste for parties, moves into the penthouse and starts hosting karaoke nights. The couple are invited, and something wakes up in both of them.

What looks like a light comedy about ageing turns into something sharper. Moshe Rosenthal's debut is about desire, visibility and what happens when two people who stopped seeing each other suddenly want to be seen by someone else. Tuvia and Yaffa both start competing for Itzik's attention, each in their own way, and the film watches them with affection but no mercy. The humour is gentle, then awkward, then quietly painful.

The karaoke sequences are genuinely electric. Rosenthal understands that singing in front of others, badly or well, is a way of saying what you can't say in conversation, and the songs carry real feeling. Lior Ashkenazi is perfect as the neighbour, magnetic and slightly hollow, and the two leads are wonderful as a couple rediscovering vanity late in life. It won Best Film at the Jerusalem Film Festival. Small, funny and surprisingly moving.
