Elias, a young man from somewhere east of Europe, jumps off a smuggling boat as it is raided by the coast guard and washes up on a Greek island, straight onto the beach of a luxury resort. To blend in, he grabs a uniform and joins the staff. A magician performing at the resort, impressed by the boy, tells him to look him up if he ever comes to Paris. From that moment Paris becomes his mythical destination.

Riccardo Scamarcio plays Elias almost as a silent-film hero, with few words and a lot of improvisation. Costa-Gavras, best known for unsparing political thrillers like Z and Missing, takes a lighter touch here: a Chaplinesque modern Odyssey that crosses contemporary Europe through its back doors. Elias meets tourists, factory bosses, lonely women, cops, fellow migrants and kind strangers. Each encounter is an episode, some comic, some cruel, and all of them say something about how Europe treats people who arrive without papers.

What the film understands is that for those without papers, the continent that calls itself the home of civilisation is mostly a series of walls. The warmth and absurdity are earned, not naive, because the danger never fully goes away. Costa-Gavras, Greek by birth and French by career, is making a film about his own two homes. A minor work in his filmography, maybe, but a generous one.
