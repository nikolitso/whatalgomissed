Giorgio is a well-off law student in Bari, serious, polite, with a good future ahead and a girlfriend his parents approve of. Then he meets Francesco, a charming, dangerous card cheat who sees in him the perfect partner: an innocent face that nobody at the table will suspect. Giorgio is flattered, then thrilled. The money, the nights, the women and the sense of being someone else pull him deeper into Francesco's world, and what starts as a game becomes something much uglier.

Daniele Vicari adapts Gianrico Carofiglio's novel and makes a study in class, moral corruption, and how quickly a person can lose themselves in someone else's orbit. Its real subject is temptation: the pleasure of crossing a line, and then the next one. Vicari films Bari at night as a place of smoky rooms, empty streets and endless possibility, which is exactly how it looks to Giorgio.

Elio Germano is very good as Giorgio, slowly losing the softness in his face. But the film belongs to Riccardo Scamarcio, magnetic and unnerving as Francesco. It is one of those performances that makes you understand exactly how the character fails while still finding him compelling, and it reminds you that Scamarcio is more than a pin-up. A dark, gripping Italian drama that deserves a wider audience.
