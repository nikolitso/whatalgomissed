Jimmy invites his closest friends to his beach house to celebrate his 45th birthday. There is food, drink, music and old jokes, the familiar rituals of men who have known each other for a long time. Then he tells them he plans to end his life before the weekend is over. Nobody knows whether to believe him, and nobody knows what to say.

It is one of the most uncomfortable premises in recent cinema, and Ariel Escalante Meza handles it with real intelligence. He doesn't turn it into a thriller about whether Jimmy will go through with it, and he doesn't offer easy explanations. Instead he watches the group. Some friends get angry. Some try to argue. Some pretend it is a bad joke and keep drinking. The beautiful setting, the sea, the sun, the house, makes it all feel even stranger, a holiday that has turned into a vigil.

The film is really about male friendship: what men will and won't say to each other when one of them is in real trouble. These friends can talk for hours about nothing, but they don't have the words for pain, and the film is merciless about that silence without being cruel to the people trapped in it. It is not easy to watch, and it isn't meant to be. A small film from Central America, rarely seen in Europe, and well worth finding. Brave, sad and honest.
