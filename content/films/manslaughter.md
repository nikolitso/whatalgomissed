Carsten is a respected teacher in his fifties with a comfortable Copenhagen life: a long marriage, a nice flat, opinions about politics he no longer acts on. He is also having an affair with Pil, a former student and a young left-wing activist. When one of her group's actions goes wrong and a policeman is killed, Carsten's carefully structured existence begins to collapse. The question is no longer about her. It is about him, and what his principles are actually worth.

Jesper Christensen is superb in the lead, a man who has always talked about commitment and is now asked to show some. Per Fly directs with no tricks at all: plain rooms, long conversations, faces under pressure. The film never tells you what Carsten should do. It just watches him make choices, one after another, each one a little more costly.

This is the third and final part of Fly's class trilogy, which completes his portrait of Danish society from the bottom (The Bench) through the middle (Manslaughter) to the top (The Inheritance). Each film shows how class shapes not just circumstance but character, and this one is the most uncomfortable because it is about the people most likely to be watching it: educated, liberal, sure of their own decency. It won the Nordic Council Film Prize. Serious, adult Danish cinema, and still underrated.
