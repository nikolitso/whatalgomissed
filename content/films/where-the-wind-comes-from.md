Alyssa is nineteen, rebellious and restless. Mehdi is twenty-three, shy, introverted and her best friend since childhood. In Tunis, with few jobs and fewer prospects, the two of them escape the only way they can: through their imagination and their dreams of somewhere else. Mehdi is the talented one, a gifted artist who draws all the time. Then Alyssa finds a drawing contest in Djerba, in the south of the country, that could open the door to a new life in Europe, and she decides Mehdi has to enter. They have no money and no real plan. They set off anyway.

Amel Guellaty made this as her first feature, and it is full of energy and invention. The road trip across Tunisia is funny and unpredictable, and the film never loses sight of the imaginary world the two friends share, the one that has kept them going all these years. Eya Bellagha is wonderful as Alyssa, fierce, impatient and very funny, and Slim Baccar gives Mehdi a quiet tenderness that makes him impossible not to love.

Underneath the adventure, it is a film about friendship, and about a generation of young Tunisians who feel that their future is somewhere else. The journey tests their friendship in ways neither of them expects. It premiered in the World Cinema Dramatic Competition at Sundance. Warm, lively and moving, and a fresh new voice from Tunisia.
