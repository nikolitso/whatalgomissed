Takis is trapped. Takis tries to escape. Takis wants money. He's got a family, a baby, a home. Takis is being cheated. He loves her and suffers. His boss tops them all. Takis has run out of time. That is the whole film in nine sentences, and Economides spends nearly two hours making you feel every one of them.

Takis works in the grey edges of Athens, the warehouses and back offices and cheap apartments that never appear in a tourist brochure. Everyone around him is shouting, lying or taking a cut. Vangelis Mourikis, who would become Economides' key actor, plays him as a man with a fuse that keeps getting shorter. The dialogue is the director's trademark: a torrent of swearing, insults and threats, so relentless that it becomes a kind of music. The camera stays close and the edits never give you air.

Economides' second feature, after Matchbox, premiered at Cannes Critics' Week and pushed his style to its absolute limit. It is a portrait of a working-class man being crushed from every direction at once, with nowhere to go and no language left except rage. Some people will find it too much, and they have a point. But nobody else in Greek cinema was showing the country this way in 2006. The film is exhausting in the best possible sense: it doesn't let you rest because Takis can't.
