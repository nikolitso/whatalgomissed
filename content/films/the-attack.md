Amin Jaafari is a well-integrated Arab-Israeli surgeon in Tel Aviv: successful, apolitical, happy. The night he receives a prestigious award, a suicide bomber blows up a restaurant full of people, and Amin spends hours operating on the wounded. Then the police call him in. The evidence points to his wife, Siham, who was supposed to be visiting family. He refuses to believe it.

Ziad Doueiri adapts Yasmina Khadra's novel, and he turns it into a kind of investigation. Desperate to understand, Amin travels to Nablus in the West Bank to find those who recruited her, and discovers that he belongs nowhere. To the Israelis he is now a suspect. To the Palestinians he is a man who chose the other side. Ali Suliman is superb as Amin, holding the film together with a performance of controlled shock, and Doueiri tells the story through flashbacks that slowly make Siham a real person rather than a headline.

It is Doueiri's most provocative film. It was banned in Lebanon and by the Arab League because he filmed in Israel with Israeli actors, which says a lot about the very borders the film is about. Doueiri, who made West Beirut and later The Insult, refuses to take sides, which is exactly why both sides found it hard to accept. It doesn't give answers, only questions that hurt. A man who loses his country twice.
