Kostis is a 40-year-old doctor who arrives on the small island of Antiparos to run the local clinic. Winter: empty, quiet, manageable. He eats alone and settles into the slow rhythm of the off-season. Then summer arrives with its crowds, its bodies, its noise, and a young tourist, Anna, walks into his surgery. She lets him tag along with her group of friends. He doesn't chase her so much as get swept away.

The locals watch. The mayor smiles. The island has seen this before. Papadimitropoulos shoots the summer sequences with genuine sensory intensity: the heat, the music, the alcohol, the nude beaches, the nights that never seem to end. For a while you are as intoxicated as Kostis is, and the film is honest enough to make that uncomfortable. Makis Papadimitriou is brilliant in the role, soft, awkward and needy, a man nobody has ever looked at twice who suddenly thinks he has been chosen.

Then the season turns, and slowly the film turns into something else entirely. Papadimitropoulos, who made Wasted Youth before this, never judges his characters from a safe distance; he puts you right next to them. It won Best Film and Best Director at the Hellenic Film Academy Awards. By the end it is one of the most uncomfortable portraits of male delusion in recent Greek cinema. You will not look at a Cycladic summer the same way again.
