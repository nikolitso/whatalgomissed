Ane and Thomas are in the middle of a divorce. The decision has been made, the conversations are mostly practical, and both are already half living their next lives. Then Ane suffers a sudden stroke. Unable to manage on her own, she needs someone at home, and Thomas agrees to stay until she recovers. It is a practical arrangement. It quickly becomes something far more complicated.

Jeanette Nordahl, who made a strong debut with the crime family drama Wildland, works here with great delicacy in the space between what is said and what is felt. Ane has to relearn simple things, and the film is honest about how humiliating that can be. Thomas is caring, but also tired, guilty and not quite sure why he is still there. Old resentments resurface at the kitchen table. Small moments of tenderness appear where neither expected them. Nordahl never tells us what the couple should do, and never turns the illness into a lesson.

The performances are exceptional, restrained and precise, the kind of acting where a glance across the room carries the weight of years. The film looks plain and lives in ordinary rooms, but nothing in it is careless. It premiered at the Berlinale in 2025, and it confirms Nordahl as one of the most interesting new voices in Danish cinema. A quiet, precise film about how illness interrupts even the endings we have decided upon.
