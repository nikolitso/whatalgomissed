Sara is seventeen, and she has grown up as a Jehovah's Witness in a close, devout family. She knocks on doors with her father, believes what she has been taught, and has never really questioned it. Then she meets Teis at a party, a young man from outside the faith, and falls secretly in love. In her community that is not a small transgression. If she chooses him, she risks being cut off from everyone she has ever known, including her own family, which is already cracking for other reasons.

Based on a true story, and handled with far more nuance than the premise suggests. Oplev neither demonizes the community nor pretends the choice is simple. The elders are strict, sometimes cruel, but the film also shows what the faith gives Sara: certainty, belonging, a family that eats together and prays together. Rosalinde Mynster is excellent as Sara, torn between two kinds of love, and a young Pilou Asbæk, before Borgen and Game of Thrones, plays Teis with warmth and patience.

Oplev, who would go on to direct the original Girl with the Dragon Tattoo, shows here the careful, empathetic observational style that makes Danish social realism so trustworthy. No big speeches, no melodrama, just a family under pressure and a girl trying to work out what she believes. It is a modest film, but an honest and very moving one about faith, family and the cost of leaving.
