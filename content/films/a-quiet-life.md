Rosario is an Italian who runs a hotel restaurant in the German countryside. He has a German wife, a young son, a kitchen he is proud of, and a calm, respectable life. He also has a past. Years ago, under another name, he was part of the Camorra world near Naples, and he walked away from it in the only way possible: by disappearing. Then two young men turn up at his door. One of them is the son he left behind.

Claudio Cupellini builds the film like a slow thriller, but its real subject is not crime. It is how completely the past refuses to stay buried, and the particular cruelty of being found by someone who still loves you. The young men are there on business, and the business is not pretty. Rosario has to decide how much of his new life he is willing to risk, and how much of his old self is still inside him.

Toni Servillo is terrifying in his stillness. Most of the time he is a polite host in an apron, and then a single look tells you who he used to be. Servillo won Best Actor at the Rome Film Festival for the role, and it sits comfortably next to his work for Sorrentino and in Gomorrah. Italian cinema has made many mafia films. Few are this restrained, or this sad. A tight, grown-up genre film with a cold heart and a very good chef.
