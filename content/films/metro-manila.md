Oscar and Mai Ramirez farm rice in the mountains of northern Luzon, and the harvest no longer pays. They take their two daughters and travel to Manila, where they are cheated within days and end up in the slums. Mai finds work in a bar. Oscar, a former soldier, lands a job with an armoured truck company, moving cash around the city. His new partner Ong is friendly, generous and a little too interested in him.

Sean Ellis is a British director, but he made the film in Tagalog, with a Filipino cast, shot entirely on location, and it never feels like an outsider's view. The city is shown as a machine that eats newcomers: everyone has a scheme, every kindness has a price. Jake Macapagal is very good as Oscar, a decent man who slowly understands that decency is a luxury here. John Arcilla makes Ong both warm and frightening.

Metro Manila is a thriller built from economic desperation. The armoured truck sequences are among the most tense in recent cinema, partly because Ellis takes his time and lets you understand exactly how much is at stake for a man who cannot afford to lose this job. It won the World Cinema Audience Award at Sundance and was nominated for a BAFTA. It holds back its last moves well, so go in knowing as little as possible. A genre film with real anger in it.
