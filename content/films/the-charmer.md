Esmail is a young Iranian man living in Denmark on a deadline. Without residency rights, he spends his nights in upmarket bars, systematically pursuing relationships with Danish women who could secure his legal status. He is good at it: polite, attentive, careful. As his deadline approaches, he meets Sara, a Danish-Iranian woman, and genuinely falls for her. Then his past follows him.

Milad Alami's debut is a taut psychological drama about race, class and survival that never settles into comfortable moral positioning. What Esmail does is manipulative; what Denmark does to him is also manipulative. The film holds both truths simultaneously. Ardalan Esmaili is very good in the lead, all charm on the surface and exhaustion underneath, and Alami shoots Copenhagen as a cool, elegant city that is always slightly out of his reach. The scenes with the women are uncomfortable precisely because nobody is a villain.

It is a quieter film than its premise suggests, closer to a character study than a thriller, and when the final act tips into genre territory, it loses a little of its grip. But it asks hard questions about who gets to belong in a wealthy European country, and on what terms, and it refuses to give easy answers. Alami later made Opponent in Sweden, and he is a director to keep watching. Uneven, but sharp and memorable.
