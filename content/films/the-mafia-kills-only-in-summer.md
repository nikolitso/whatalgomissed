Arturo is born in Palermo in the early seventies, and from the start his life runs alongside the Mafia's. As a boy he is in love with a classmate named Flora, obsessed with Giulio Andreotti, and convinced by the adults around him that the killings in the news are just quarrels over women, or something that happens when it's hot. The film follows him through the 1970s and 80s, the years of the Mafia's most visible violence, told by the grown man looking back on a childhood lived in its shadow.

Pif, a television satirist making his first feature, plays the adult Arturo himself and manages to be both funny and heartbreaking. He treats a subject usually handled with solemnity as something that was simply there, part of the weather. Real figures pass through the edge of Arturo's life: judges, policemen, journalists, the men who stood up and paid for it. At first they are background characters in a childish romantic comedy. Slowly, the background takes over, and the comedy turns into something else.

That shift is what makes the film. Pif understands that for a whole generation of Palermitans, the Mafia wasn't a movie genre but a silence, and his real subject is how people learned to break it. It won the European Film Award for Best Comedy, and it is a rare film that earns both the laughs and the tears. A light touch, and a very serious heart.
