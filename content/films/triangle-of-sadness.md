It starts with a casting call for male models, and a joke about the difference between an H&M smile and a Balenciaga frown. Carl, a model, and Yaya, his influencer girlfriend, argue about who pays for dinner. Then they board a luxury cruise she got for free. On deck: billionaires, a Russian oligarch who made his fortune in fertiliser, an elderly British couple who sell weapons, and a staff trained to say yes to everything. A captain, played by Woody Harrelson, who would rather drink than leave his cabin.

Östlund dismantles class, capitalism and the stories we tell ourselves about merit. He makes you laugh while he does it, and then makes you uncomfortable about having laughed. The captain's dinner, held in a storm, is already famous: a Marxist captain and a capitalist oligarch trading quotes over the ship's speakers while the guests are sick around them. It is crude, yes. It is also very precise.

The third act is where it becomes genuinely great. On a deserted island, the only person who knows how to fish and make a fire is Abigail, the cleaning lady, played magnificently by Dolly de Leon, and suddenly the hierarchy turns upside down. At two and a half hours it is a little long, and Östlund likes to repeat a point. But it is his funniest and angriest film. It won him his second Palme d'Or at Cannes in 2022, after The Square, and three Oscar nominations. Satire with real teeth.
