Felice Lasco left Naples as a teenager and has spent forty years in Egypt, where he became a successful businessman, a husband and a convert to Islam. Now he returns to the Rione Sanità, the old neighbourhood that formed him, to see his ageing mother. He means to stay a short time. Instead he finds himself walking the same alleys again and again, looking for something, and for someone: Oreste, his closest childhood friend, who stayed behind and went a very different way.

Mario Martone adapts Ermanno Rea's novel with patience. The first half is almost a documentary of return: Felice relearning the dialect, eating the food, sitting with a local priest who works with the neighbourhood's kids. Pierfrancesco Favino is superb, a man who speaks Neapolitan with an Arabic accent and seems foreign everywhere, even to himself. The scenes with his mother are some of the most tender of his career.

It is a film about the places that never let you go, the identities we leave behind and find waiting when we return. And about the past as something not just remembered but owed. Martone, a Neapolitan himself, shoots Naples with the intimacy of someone who has never quite left: no postcards, just stairways, crypts and crowded streets. It played in competition at Cannes. Slow, but it gets under your skin.
