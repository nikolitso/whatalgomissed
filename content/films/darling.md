Darling is a world-famous Danish ballerina who has made her career abroad. She comes home to Copenhagen to dance Giselle, the role that will crown everything. During rehearsals she collapses. Her hip is irreparably damaged, and she will never dance again. Rather than walk away, she begins training her young replacement, a girl who has everything she had, including the relentlessness that destroyed her.

The film is set inside the cold institutional beauty of professional ballet: mirrored studios, pale light, bodies pushed past pain as a matter of routine. It shows the discipline without romanticising it. Danica Curcic is fierce in the lead, a woman whose body was her whole language, suddenly struck dumb. Her marriage starts to bend under the weight.

This is a film about what happens when identity and vocation are the same thing and one is taken away. What is left of a dancer who cannot dance? Control, mostly, and the urge to live through someone else. It can feel chilly, and the drama sometimes stays at arm's length when it should hurt. But the central question is a good one, and the dance sequences are gorgeous. Worth seeing for anyone who loved Black Swan but wanted something less hysterical and more grown up.
