Julie is a single mother of two who lives in a small town outside Paris and works as head chambermaid in a luxury hotel in the city. Every day starts before dawn and ends after dark, with a long commute, a neighbour minding the children, and an ex-husband who doesn't send the money. She has a chance at a better job, in her own field, and an interview to get to. Then a general transport strike paralyses the region.

Éric Gravel shoots it like a thriller: relentless, claustrophobic, scored with pounding electronica. Trains are cancelled, buses don't come, she hitches lifts and runs between appointments, and the hotel keeps calling. It is a film about how just staying financially afloat can feel like a white-knuckle survival exercise. There are no villains here, only a system where one missed train can unravel everything.

Laure Calamy, best known from Call My Agent!, won Best Actress in the Horizons section at Venice, and Gravel took Best Director. It is a performance of sustained, exhausting credibility. You never catch her acting, only coping. The film's limitation is its own intensity: at times it feels like one long chase with the same note held. But that note is true, and anyone who has ever juggled a job, a commute and children on their own will recognise every second. Short, sharp and stressful in the best sense.
