Franco Amore has been a Milan police officer for thirty-five years and has never once fired his gun. Tonight is his last night on the job. His wife Viviana is organising a surprise party, and Franco has taken on a quiet side job for a little extra money: security for Chinese couriers moving diamonds across the city. It should be easy. It isn't. By morning a friend is dead, and Franco is lying to everyone, including the people he is supposed to report to.

Andrea Di Stefano, who made The Informer before this, opens with a spectacular aerial sweep over Milan at night and never really lets go. The story moves back and forth across the same hours, and the city is shot as somewhere glittering and cold, all ring roads and empty offices. It is stylish Italian noir with a thriller's grip and a genuine moral centre, closer to Heat than anything Italy has produced in years.

Pierfrancesco Favino carries it with the quiet authority of a man who has spent a career playing men at the edge of what they are capable of. Here he plays an honest cop discovering how far he will go to save himself, and you can see the calculation on his face scene by scene. Linda Caridi is excellent as Viviana, much more than the worried wife. It premiered at the Berlinale and did well at home. Proof that Italian genre cinema is very much alive.
