Salma Zidane is a Palestinian widow who lives alone on the West Bank, right on the line, tending the lemon grove her father planted fifty years ago. Then the new Israeli Defence Minister moves into the house next door. The security services decide her trees are a threat, a place where gunmen could hide, and order them cut down. Salma, who has never made a fuss about anything, hires a young lawyer and takes the case all the way to the Supreme Court.

Hiam Abbass plays her with enormous dignity, almost without words. Across the fence, the minister's wife Mira watches from her big glass house and slowly realises she has more in common with the woman next door than with her husband. Two women on opposite sides of a wall, both trapped by the men around them. The film is inspired by a real case, and Eran Riklis, who made The Syrian Bride, keeps the politics small and human. A grove, a fence, a watchtower, a court. That is enough.

There is humour here too, a dry absurdity in the soldiers guarding the trees and the media circus that gathers around a widow and her lemons. Riklis never pretends a court case can fix what is wrong, and he doesn't turn either side into cartoons. It won the Audience Award in the Panorama section at the Berlinale. A small film about a big conflict, and better for it.
