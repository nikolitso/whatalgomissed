Cleuza is a housekeeper in the poorer outskirts of São Paulo, a single mother raising four sons by different fathers, with a fifth child on the way. She is a fanatical Corinthians supporter, and she is tired. Her boys are each trying to find a way out. Dario dreams of football, but he is about to turn eighteen and every trial is a last chance. Dinho has found God and works at a petrol station. Dênis is a motorcycle courier with a baby of his own and no money. The youngest, Reginaldo, rides the city buses looking for the father he has never met.

Walter Salles and Daniela Thomas are at their most humanistic here. After Central Station and The Motorcycle Diaries, Salles comes home to a São Paulo of traffic jams, crowded buses and endless concrete, and shoots it with real affection. Football is the thread, but survival is the subject. Each brother gets his own small story, and none of them is resolved neatly. The film trusts that their lives matter even without a big plot.

São Paulo in all its brutal, beautiful complexity. Sandra Corveloni, a theatre actress little known in cinema, won Best Actress at Cannes for her portrayal of the mother holding it all together, and she deserved it: tough, funny, exhausted, never asking for pity. Quieter and rougher than the films Salles is known for, and maybe more honest.
