Amanda is a university professor in Newcastle. Her mother Leslie has advanced dementia and lives in London with Amanda's stepfather Martin, who has cared for her devotedly. One day Amanda walks in on them in bed together. Leslie's doctor has already told Martin that she can no longer consent. Shaken and angry, Amanda calls the police, and Martin is arrested. Then she moves into her parents' house with her teenage daughter to look after her mother herself, and discovers how much more complicated the situation is than she thought.

Lance Hammer builds the film with great patience and restraint, and he never tells you what to think. Martin's love for his wife is real, and so is Amanda's fear for her mother. Leslie, meanwhile, can no longer say what she wants, but she is still a person with feelings and desires of her own. Juliette Binoche is excellent as Amanda, a woman trying to do the right thing without being sure what it is. Tom Courtenay and Anna Calder-Marshall are extraordinary as the elderly couple.

It is a film about love at the very end of life, about autonomy and protection, and about the impossible decisions families face when someone they love is disappearing in front of them. It is quiet and often uncomfortable, and it asks difficult questions without easy answers. Tender, honest and deeply humane.
