Giovanna works long shifts at a poultry plant in Rome, has two small children, and is stuck in a greying marriage with a husband she barely talks to any more. Her only escape is the window across the courtyard, where a handsome young man lives, and she watches him in the evenings. Then her husband brings home an elderly stranger he found wandering the streets, a man who can't remember who he is. Reluctantly, Giovanna begins to care for him.

The old man turns out to be a Holocaust survivor, and his story reaches back to the roundup of Rome's Jews in 1943. Ferzan Özpetek, the Turkish-born director who made his career in Italy, handles the two time threads with great delicacy. The past never feels like a history lesson. It feels like a warning addressed directly to Giovanna: about the lives we don't allow ourselves to live, and what we lose by waiting.

Giovanna Mezzogiorno is superb, tired and luminous at once, and Raoul Bova plays the neighbour with more restraint than his heartthrob status suggested. Massimo Girotti, a veteran of Visconti's Ossessione, plays the old man in what turned out to be his last role. It won several David di Donatello Awards, including Best Film. A quiet film about desire, memory and regret, and one of Özpetek's best.
