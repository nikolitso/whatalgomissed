Nisha is sixteen and living two lives: the perfect Pakistani daughter at home, a normal Norwegian teenager everywhere else. She goes to parties, plays basketball and has a boyfriend her family knows nothing about. When her father catches the boy in her bedroom, both worlds collapse at once. The Norwegian authorities get involved, the community starts talking, and her family does what they think they have to do. She is taken to Pakistan and left there with relatives.

Iram Haq based the film on her own experience: as a teenager she was taken from Oslo to Pakistan by her family. That gives it an anger and a precision that no outsider could fake. But she refuses easy villains. Nisha's father, played by Adil Hussain with great complexity, loves his daughter and is terrified of losing face, and the film makes you feel both. The title is the question that rules his life.

At its core it is about the racism of belonging, not the overt kind, but the kind that tells a person they are never quite right in either place they call home. In Norway Nisha is too Pakistani, in Pakistan too Norwegian, and the newcomer Maria Mozhdah carries the whole film on her face. It premiered at Toronto and won the Amanda for Best Film. Angry, precise and deeply empathetic, and the scenes in Pakistan are among the most frightening I have seen in a family drama.
