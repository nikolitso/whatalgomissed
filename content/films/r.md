Rune, a young man barely out of his teens, arrives at Denmark's toughest prison and is immediately reduced to a letter. He is just another inmate in a system that has no interest in him as a person. Within days he has learned the hierarchy: who runs the corridor, who you pay, who you must never cross. To survive he starts running errands for the men above him, and every favour buys him a little more trouble.

Tobias Lindholm and Michael Noer co-directed it as their debut, shot in the decommissioned Horsens State Prison with a cast of ex-convicts alongside professional actors. The result is a documentary realism that is genuinely unsettling. There is almost no music, no visual flourish, just the clang of doors and the sound of men waiting. Pilou Asbæk, years before Borgen and Game of Thrones made him famous, is outstanding as Rune: watchful, scared, trying to look harder than he is.

No escape, no redemption arc, no manipulation. Just the institution working exactly as designed, grinding the people inside it into whatever shape it requires. Lindholm went on to write The Hunt and direct A Hijacking and A War, and you can see his method here already: procedure as drama, systems as villains. A tough, honest film that says more about prison in ninety minutes than most say in a whole series.
