A people smuggler, working the routes that bring refugees from the Middle East into Europe, ends up responsible for a twelve-year-old Syrian girl who has been separated from her family somewhere along the way. He has every reason to get rid of her. She is a risk, a delay, a cost. Instead they set off together towards Sweden, through borders, safe houses and the hands of people who are not always safe at all.

Abbe Hassan builds it as a road movie that refuses both sentimentality and despair. It treats immigration not as a political issue but as a human one: what it costs, who pays it, and what unexpected grace sometimes appears along the way. The journey is shown in concrete detail, the money changing hands, the waiting, the fear of every checkpoint, without turning it into a catalogue of suffering.

The relationship at the centre is what holds it together. Hassan doesn't make the smuggler a hero or a villain. He makes him a person, which is more difficult and more interesting. He is mercenary and tired, and he is also capable of decency, and the film lets both be true. The girl, sharp and stubborn, is no passive victim either. It opened the Göteborg Film Festival, a fitting showcase for a new voice in Swedish cinema. Modest in scale, but honest, and it moves you without manipulating you.
