The Kim family live in a semi-basement flat in Seoul, folding pizza boxes for cash and stealing wi-fi from the neighbours. Then the son, Ki-woo, gets a chance to tutor the daughter of the wealthy Park family, armed with a forged diploma. Soon his sister is the Parks' art therapist, his father their driver and his mother their housekeeper, one member at a time, each posing as an unrelated professional, each one pushing out the person who had the job before.

Bong Joon-ho builds the first half as a dark comedy of class aspiration, precise and very funny, then pulls the floor out completely. He has been making films about class since Memories of Murder, The Host and Snowpiercer, but never with this control. Every camera movement and every staircase means something. The house itself is the film's real argument: who gets to live above ground, who gets buried below, and the violence required to maintain that arrangement. Watch what he does with rain, and with smell.

Song Kang-ho is wonderful as the father, a man swallowing humiliations until he can't. The whole cast moves like a single machine. It won the Palme d'Or unanimously, and then became the first film not in English to win the Oscar for Best Picture, among four Oscars in total. It deserves the hype, which is rare. Go in knowing as little as possible.
