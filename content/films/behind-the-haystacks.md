Northern Greece, by the lake that marks the border with North Macedonia. Stergios is drowning in debt, and to keep his family afloat he starts helping smugglers move refugees across the border. His wife Maria finds her refuge in church and faith. Their daughter Eleni is caught between them, in a small community where everybody knows everybody's business. Then a tragic incident befalls the family, the kind that happens and then disappears from the news.

Asimina Proedrou's debut tells the story in three parts, each from the point of view of one of the three family members, and the same events take on new meaning every time. It is restrained in a way that takes real confidence: no music manipulating your emotions, no dramatic confrontations, just the slow accumulation of consequence. The lake, flat and grey and beautiful, watches over everything. The refugees are mostly glimpsed, silent figures in the dark, which is exactly how the locals choose to see them.

It is a film about the weight of a decision and who ends up carrying it, and for how long. It is also about guilt, religion and the small hypocrisies of a village that would rather look away. Proedrou won Best Film at Thessaloniki, and the film went on to sweep the Hellenic Film Academy Awards, including Best Film. One of the most assured Greek debuts in years.
