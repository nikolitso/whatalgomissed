Dr Kaveh Nariman is a forensic pathologist in Tehran. Driving home one night, his car clips a motorbike carrying a man, his wife and their two children. The eight-year-old son seems only lightly hurt. Nariman offers to take him to a clinic and pay for everything; the father, Moosa, proud and suspicious, refuses. A few days later the boy dies, and his body arrives at the morgue where Nariman works. The autopsy points to another cause entirely. The question is whether Nariman can believe it.

Vahid Jalilvand builds a moral thriller out of silence and professional distance: the gap between what a man knows and what he can bring himself to do. Amir Aghaei plays Nariman with the stillness of someone used to looking at death without feeling it, now unable to stop feeling. Navid Mohammadzadeh is overwhelming as Moosa, a poor man whose grief comes out as rage, and whose own guilt runs deeper than he can admit.

The class dynamic between the comfortable doctor and the working-class family runs underneath everything, quietly indicting a system where dignity costs more than justice. Like the best Iranian cinema since Farhadi, it puts ordinary people in front of an impossible ethical choice and refuses to make it easier. In the Orizzonti section at Venice it won Best Director, and Mohammadzadeh won Best Actor. Grey, tense and unforgettable.
