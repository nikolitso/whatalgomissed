A mother in the middle of a bitter custody battle, worried about how her children are coping with their father, decides she cannot wait for the court any longer. She takes her children and flies to Tenerife without telling anyone, calling it a holiday. Sun, a hotel pool, a few days of being a family again. Except the children know something is wrong, and so do we.

Amanda Kernell, who made the remarkable Sami Blood, refuses to turn this into a thriller. There are no chases, no police at the door. Instead she studies a love that has crossed the line into something else: desperation, obsession, the inability to accept a loss that is also a reasonable legal outcome. The children's reactions are the film's sharpest instrument. They are wary, loyal, protective of a parent who should be protecting them, and the gaps in their trust are where the drama happens.

Kernell never judges her heroine, but never exonerates her either. We understand why she does it, and we understand why it is wrong. The holiday resort, all bright light and forced fun, becomes the perfect setting for a family pretending nothing has happened. It won four Guldbagge Awards, including Best Director. Quiet, unsettling, and harder to watch than films that announce their difficulty. It stays under your skin.
