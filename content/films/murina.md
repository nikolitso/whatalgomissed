Julija is a teenager on a rocky Croatian island, spending the summer with her parents. She dives for moray eels with her father Ante, a hard, controlling man who treats his wife and daughter as part of his property. Then Javier arrives, an old family friend, rich, charming and worldly, whom Ante hopes to sell their land to. For Julija he is a door to somewhere else. For her mother, perhaps a memory of another life. And Ante watches all of it.

The film is shot off the Dalmatian coast in water so clear it almost hurts, and the beauty and the suffocation are inseparable. Antoneta Alamat Kusijanović keeps the camera close to Julija's body, in and out of the sea, and builds tension out of glances, heat and small cruelties rather than events. Gracija Filipović is remarkable as Julija, fierce and silent. Leon Lučev makes Ante frightening without ever making him a monster, and Cliff Curtis gives Javier just the right ambiguity.

This was Kusijanović's first feature, grown out of her earlier short film, and it was executive produced by Martin Scorsese. It won the Caméra d'Or for best first feature at Cannes in 2021, a landmark moment for Croatian cinema, and took Best Film at Pula. A coming-of-age story, a family thriller and a postcard turned inside out. The sea has rarely looked so beautiful or so dangerous.
