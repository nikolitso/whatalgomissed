Lorenzo is an elderly retired lawyer in Naples, recovering from a health scare and living alone. He has little time for his adult children, and they have learned to expect little from him. Then a young couple with two small children move in next door, and Lorenzo, who has always been cold to his own family, finds himself drawn into their lives, offering small kindnesses he never offered at home.

Gianni Amelio, the director of Stolen Children and Lamerica, adapts a novel by Lorenzo Marone, and he builds the film slowly and precisely, with his characteristic patience. Naples here is not the postcard city or the Gomorrah city, but a place of quiet streets and tall apartment buildings where people live close together and still know nothing about each other. Elio Germano and Micaela Ramazzotti play the neighbours, and Giovanna Mezzogiorno is excellent as the daughter who has given up waiting for her father's affection.

It is a film about the warmth we withhold and the specific damage that causes over a lifetime, about the tenderness we find easier to offer strangers than our own blood. Halfway through it takes a darker turn that I won't spoil. Renato Carpentieri is magnificent: gruff, funny, selfish and, in the end, heartbreaking. A wise, mature film from a master of Italian cinema, and far too little seen outside Italy.
