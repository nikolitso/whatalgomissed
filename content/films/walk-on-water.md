Eyal is a Mossad hitman, very good at his job and reeling from his wife's suicide, which he refuses to discuss. His next assignment is gentler on paper: track down an aging Nazi war criminal who may still be alive, by posing as a tour guide for the man's grandchildren. Axel has come to Israel to visit his sister Pia, who lives on a kibbutz. He is German, curious, open and gay, and over a few days of driving around the country he slowly dismantles every wall Eyal has built around himself.

Eytan Fox uses the thriller format to ask deeper questions about Israeli identity, masculinity and inherited trauma. Eyal carries his family's history of the Holocaust and was raised to be hard, and Axel is the grandson of a perpetrator who wants nothing to do with his family's past. The film puts these two men in a car together and lets them get on each other's nerves. Lior Ashkenazi is excellent as Eyal, a man whose toughness is a kind of illness, and Knut Berger gives Axel a warmth that never feels naive.

Fox, who had already made Yossi & Jagger and would go on to make The Bubble, was one of the first Israeli directors to put gay characters at the centre of mainstream films. It won the Audience Award in the Panorama section at the Berlinale and found a big international audience. Not every turn of the plot convinces. But the friendship at its heart does, and that is what you remember.
