Mariel is a veteran elite diver with one last chance at the Olympic Games. She has given her body and her youth to the sport, and her relationship with her coach is close, almost familial. Then she is paired for synchronised diving with a much younger girl, and a terrible truth comes to light about the coach and the girl. Mariel faces the biggest question of her career: is winning her true dream, and what is she willing to ignore to get there?

Lucía Puenzo, the Argentine director of XXY, has always been drawn to bodies under pressure and the secrets people keep about them. Here she brings that unflinching eye to the abuse of power in elite sport. The pool sequences are cold, clinical and beautiful, all tiles and silence and the slap of water, and the training regime is shown with no glamour at all. The real drama happens in the corridors and hotel rooms, where everyone knows a little and nobody says much.

What makes it more than an issue film is that Puenzo focuses on complicity. Mariel is not a bystander; she was once the girl, and the system that made her is the one she now has to judge. The film asks what silence costs the people forced to keep it, and gives no easy answers. Tough, focused and necessary.
