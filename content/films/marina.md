Rocco Granata was the son of a Calabrian coal miner who moved his family to Belgium in the years after the war, when Italian men were traded for coal and sent down the pits of Limburg. Young Rocco grows up between two worlds: an Italian home ruled by a stern, proud father, and a Belgian town that does not much want them. He discovers the accordion. His father wants him to study and get out of the mines. In 1959 Rocco writes a song called Marina, and it becomes a hit across Europe.

Belgian director Stijn Coninx, best known for the Oscar-nominated Daens, tells the story with real warmth and a good eye for period detail: the barracks where the miners live, the dances, the small humiliations of being foreign. Luigi Lo Cascio is excellent as the father, a man whose pride is the only thing he has left. The heart of the film is that relationship, a son trying to win the respect of a father who cannot say what he feels.

It is a music biopic, yes, but the song is almost the least of it. This is a film about belonging, music and the cost of having come from somewhere. Anyone with a family that left home for work will recognise something here. One of the great immigrant stories in European cinema, and almost entirely unknown outside Belgium. Find it.
