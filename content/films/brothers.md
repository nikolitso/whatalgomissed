Michael is a Danish officer with a loving wife, two daughters and a settled life. His younger brother Jannik has just come out of prison and is everything Michael is not. Then Michael is sent to Afghanistan, his helicopter goes down, and he is presumed killed. Jannik steps in to support the family, and something forms between him and his sister-in-law Sarah that neither of them planned. Then Michael comes home, and he is not the same man who left.

Susanne Bier shoots it with the handheld closeness of the Dogme years, from a script by Anders Thomas Jensen, and the cast is superb. Ulrich Thomsen, Connie Nielsen and Nikolaj Lie Kaas play it without a false note. Bier's war film is really a film about what violence does to a person, and what that person then does to everyone who loves them. The Afghan sequences are harrowing. The domestic sequences are somehow worse, because there the damage has nowhere to go but inward, into the kitchen and the children's bedroom.

It is also a film about the brothers themselves: how families assign roles early, the good son and the bad one, and how those roles can flip overnight. Jim Sheridan later remade it in Hollywood with Tobey Maguire, Jake Gyllenhaal and Natalie Portman, and it was a decent film. But the Danish original has a rawness the remake couldn't replicate. This is Bier at her very best.
