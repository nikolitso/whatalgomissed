Marco Carrera is an eye doctor whom everyone has called "the hummingbird" since childhood, because he was small for his age. The name sticks for another reason: he spends his life hovering in place, working hard just to stay where he is. The film follows him across several decades, and the losses pile up: a sister, a great love he never quite lived, a marriage, a daughter. Out of them comes a portrait of a man who has learned to live near grief without being consumed by it.

Francesca Archibugi adapts Sandro Veronesi's novel, which won the Strega Prize, and she keeps its broken chronology. The film jumps back and forth through the years, from summer houses by the sea in the seventies to Rome and beyond, and Archibugi handles time with rare delicacy. Each jump forward reveals how little and how much has changed. Faces age, the same arguments come back, and a few people stay loyal across a whole lifetime.

Pierfrancesco Favino again, and again he is excellent, carrying Marco from youth to old age without ever showing off. Bérénice Bejo is luminous as Luisa, the woman he never stops loving, Kasia Smutniak is fierce as his wife, and Nanni Moretti turns up as a psychoanalyst who becomes Marco's unlikely friend. It is a melancholic film, sometimes a little too polished, but it earns its emotions honestly. For anyone who has lived long enough to count their losses.
