Lia is a retired schoolteacher from Batumi, on Georgia's Black Sea coast. She made a promise to her late sister: to find her niece Tekla, a trans woman who left home years ago and is thought to be somewhere in Istanbul. With her travels Achi, a young man from the neighbourhood with his own reasons to escape. Neither speaks Turkish. Neither has much of a plan.

What begins as a search film, procedural and purposeful, becomes something quieter and more profound. In Istanbul they cross paths with Evrim, a trans lawyer fighting for official recognition, and the city opens up: ferries, back streets, kitchens, the communities that live on the edges. Levan Akin lets the search slow down until it is less about finding Tekla than about what Lia has to face in herself. It is about the borders we cross between who we were and who we are willing to become.

Akin, a Swedish director of Georgian roots, works with the same warmth and precision as his earlier And Then We Danced. Mzia Arabuli is wonderful as Lia: stern, stubborn, funny when she doesn't mean to be, and slowly cracking open. The film opened the Panorama section at Berlin 2024 and won the Teddy Award. Gentle without being soft, and one of the most humane films of the decade so far.
