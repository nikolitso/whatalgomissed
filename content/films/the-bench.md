Kaj is an unemployed alcoholic who spends his days on a public bench in a Copenhagen housing estate with like-minded souls, drinking beer and passing the time. He has lost almost everything and stopped pretending otherwise. When a single mother and her young son move in nearby, escaping trouble of her own, Kaj becomes involved in her life and her troubles, and slowly realises she may be connected to the life he left behind.

This was Per Fly's debut feature: unsparing, unglamorous, set entirely in the working-class world Danish cinema rarely visited. There are no picturesque streets here, just concrete, supermarkets and stairwells. Fly never looks down on his characters and never turns them into victims. The drinkers on the bench are funny, quarrelsome and loyal, and the film finds as much humanity in their small rituals as in the bigger drama that unfolds around Kaj.

The film is anchored by a towering performance from Jesper Christensen as a man who has given up on himself but not entirely on others. He is gruff, sometimes repellent, and still impossible not to root for. It was the first part of the class trilogy that would define Fly's career, followed by The Inheritance, set among the rich, and Manslaughter, among the middle class. Watch all three in order if you can. This first one is the rawest.
