Sabina works as a voice actress, dubbing films in a Rome studio. Her life looks settled: a partner, work she is good at, friends. Then she becomes pregnant, and she begins having nightmares she can't explain, fragments of a childhood she thought she remembered clearly. Her brother, who lives in America, won't speak about it. So she travels to see him.

Cristina Comencini adapts her own novel, and she handles the subject, buried abuse within a respectable family, without sensationalism and without easy resolution. This is a film about memory: what we choose to keep, what we lock away, and the courage it takes to look at what actually happened. Giovanna Mezzogiorno is excellent as Sabina, all nerves under a composed surface, and she won the Volpi Cup for Best Actress at Venice for it.

The subplots around Sabina's friends sometimes feel like a different, lighter film, and the pace can sag. But the scenes between Sabina and her brother are the reason to see it. The silence between siblings carries more weight than most films' dialogue: two adults who survived the same house and dealt with it in opposite ways. It was nominated for the Oscar for Best Foreign Language Film. Not perfect, but serious, humane and braver than most films that touch this subject.
