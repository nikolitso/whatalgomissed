Berlin, four in the morning. Victoria, a young Spanish woman who has only been in the city a few months, is leaving a nightclub when she meets four local guys. They are loud, charming, a little drunk. They wander the streets, climb onto a roof, share beers and stories. She likes them, especially Sonne. But one of them owes a debt to the wrong people, and before dawn the night turns into something none of them can control.

Sebastian Schipper shot the entire film, more than two hours, in one single continuous take, moving through twenty-two locations across Berlin. It is not a gimmick. Because there are no cuts, you live every minute with the characters, and the long, lazy first hour of flirting and drifting makes what comes after hit with frightening force. Laia Costa is incredible as Victoria, and Frederick Lau and Franz Rogowski make the guys feel completely real. The cinematographer won a Silver Bear for it.

Yes, there are moments where you notice the improvisation, and the plot needs a few improbable turns. But the experience is unlike anything else: a real night, in real time, sliding from romance into catastrophe. Exhausting and exhilarating. You will not forget it.
