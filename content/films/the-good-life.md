Four characters in Santiago, each struggling to reach a goal that keeps slipping away. A psychologist running public health campaigns can't keep her own life in order. A hairdresser in his forties, still living with his mother, wants a car and a bit of dignity. A classical musician hopes for a place in the orchestra and ends up with something much smaller. A young woman sleeps rough with her baby and simply tries to survive the day.

Andrés Wood, who made Machuca a few years earlier, builds an interlocking portrait of a city where these people brush past each other without ever really meeting. It is quiet and precise. Nobody is a hero, nobody is a villain, and life keeps not working out the way anyone planned. Wood shows a modern, prosperous-looking Santiago of traffic, billboards and shopping centres, and lets you feel the loneliness underneath the progress. The irony of the title is gentle but it is there.

What I like most is that Wood never forces the connections into a neat puzzle, the way so many ensemble films do. The links are small, often accidental, and the film trusts you to notice them. It won the Goya for Best Spanish Language Foreign Film. A modest film in the best sense, and a good door into contemporary Chilean cinema.
