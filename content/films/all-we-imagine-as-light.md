Prabha is a senior nurse at a Mumbai hospital. Her husband went to work in Germany soon after their arranged marriage and barely calls, though one day a rice cooker arrives from him without explanation. Her young roommate Anu is in love with Shiaz, a Muslim boy, and they have nowhere in the city to be alone. Parvaty, a cook at the hospital, is being forced out of her home to make way for a development. Three women, one indifferent city.

Payal Kapadia films Mumbai in the monsoon and at night: crowded trains, wet streets, neon reflected in puddles, voices of migrants who came for work and stayed without ever quite belonging. She works in a register between documentary and fiction, between waking and dreaming, and she never forces the drama. Kani Kusruti and Divya Prabha are wonderful as Prabha and Anu, two women who share a small room and very different kinds of loneliness. When the three travel together to a coastal village, something opens, in them and in the film.

Kapadia came from documentary, and her earlier A Night of Knowing Nothing won the Golden Eye at Cannes. This was the first Indian film in the Cannes competition in thirty years, and it won the Grand Prix in 2024. It is a long way from Bollywood and all the better for it. Meditative, luminous, and genuinely unforgettable.
