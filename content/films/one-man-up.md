Naples, the 1980s. Two men share a name, Antonio Pisapia, and nothing else. One is a footballer, a decent, introverted defender whose career is cut short by injury, and who dreams of becoming a coach. The other, Tony, is a famous pop singer, loud, coked-up and full of himself, who is about to fall from grace after a scandal. They barely meet. Sorrentino follows both as they navigate decline in a city that forgets fast.

This is his debut feature, and already unmistakably his: the baroque compositions, the melancholy, the interest in men whose best years are behind them. It is also the first of his films with Toni Servillo, who plays the singer with appalling, magnificent vulgarity. Andrea Renzi is quieter and more touching as the footballer. The two characters were loosely inspired by real figures, but Sorrentino is less interested in biography than in the moment when a public life turns into a private humiliation.

It is uneven. The parallel structure doesn't always pay off, and the second half drags. But there are scenes here, especially with Servillo, that are as good as anything he made later. It premiered at Venice and won Sorrentino the Nastro d'Argento for Best New Director. If you know The Consequences of Love, Il Divo or The Great Beauty, you will see the seeds of everything that follows. For Sorrentino fans, essential. For everyone else, a curiosity.
