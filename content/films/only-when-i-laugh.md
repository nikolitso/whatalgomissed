A young housewife, married with a small daughter, quietly asks her husband if she can go back and finish the university degree she abandoned when the baby came. It seems like nothing. He agrees, even seems pleased. But his support slowly curdles into control: questions about where she was, who she spoke to, how much time she spends on her books. And then into something considerably worse.

The film is based on a real Croatian court case, and Vanja Juranić treats it with restraint. There are no big scenes of violence for most of the running time, no music telling you what to fear. Instead she works in an understated register that makes the slow accumulation of menace all the more suffocating. A comment at dinner. A closed door. A smile that lasts too long. The flat becomes smaller and smaller, and you start to notice everything she notices.

It is a domestic thriller that never announces itself as one, and that is its strength. It shows how coercive control actually works: gradually, politely, with the full cooperation of a society that sees a good husband and a nice home. The title's bitter irony becomes clear as it goes on. Croatian cinema has been on a fine run lately, from Murina onwards, and this is a strong addition. Hard to watch, and important for exactly that reason.
