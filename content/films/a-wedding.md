Zahira is eighteen, Belgian, and the daughter of a Pakistani family she loves. She goes to school, has friends, and has a life her parents only partly know about. Her family has decided it is time for her to marry. Prospective husbands from Pakistan are lined up and introduced to her over Skype. Zahira does not want any of them. But this is a family, not a prison, and that is precisely what makes it so hard.

Stephan Streker's film is taut and precise. It plays like a thriller built from domestic pressure and cultural obligation, and it refuses easy villains or simple solutions. The parents are not monsters. They love their daughter and believe they are protecting her. Her older brother is torn between her and his duty to them. Even the pressure is gentle at first: tea, worried faces, appeals to honour. Lina El Arabi is remarkable as Zahira, defiant and frightened at once, a young woman trying to keep both her freedom and her family when the two can no longer live together.

Streker keeps his camera close and the tone level. He never exoticises the family or turns the story into a lecture about integration. That restraint makes the tension almost unbearable in the final stretch. It is a small, serious film that deserves a much bigger audience than it found. One of Belgian cinema's most quietly devastating recent films.
