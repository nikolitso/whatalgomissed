Reza has left Tehran for a quiet life in the north of Iran, where he runs a small goldfish farm. His wife is the headmistress of a girls' school. They have a son, a mortgage, and debts. What they also have is land that a powerful local company wants, and that company has effectively bought the police, the courts, the bank, and every institution around him. Reza refuses to bow. That is the whole story, and it is enough.

What follows is a portrait of corruption so total it becomes almost abstract. Not a conspiracy but a climate, the air everyone breathes. Bribes are not shocking here. They are expected, even polite. The man who refuses to pay is treated as the troublemaker. Rasoulof films it in cold grey light, long silences, and a landscape that seems as exhausted as its people. Reza Akhlaghirad plays him with a stubborn, wounded dignity, and you are never sure whether to admire him or fear for him.

Rasoulof makes the personal political with devastating restraint, and he paid for it. He made the film after years of trouble with the authorities, and it won the Un Certain Regard prize at Cannes in 2017. His troubles only grew afterwards, all the way to The Seed of the Sacred Fig and his exile. This is a film about what it costs to remain a decent person in a system that has decided decency is a threat. My highest-rated film on the Iranian list.
