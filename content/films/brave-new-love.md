Kate is a hypnotherapist. She lives with her husband Orla and their daughter Athena, and from the outside her life is calm and settled. But Kate also has Andreas, a lover she has kept completely secret, and the relationship is deep, romantic and very real. She is not looking for a way out of her marriage. She is looking for something harder to name: a way to love that does not force her to choose, and a life that she can show her daughter without shame.

Maria Bäck tells Kate's story as an erotic, existential comedy, and the film refuses to play by the usual rules. There is body and desire and awkwardness, there is Greek mythology woven through the story, and there is a lot of laughter, sometimes at the most uncomfortable moments. Kathrine Thorborg Johansen is excellent as Kate, curious, clumsy and brave, a woman trying to be honest in a world that has no language for what she wants. Simon Sears and Anders Danielsen Lie complete a strong cast.

It is a film about love and freedom, about the gap between the life we are told to live and the one we actually feel, and about what we teach our children by the way we love. Some will find it too open, some too strange. I found it bold, funny and surprisingly tender. A fresh, modern Nordic film that dares to ask difficult questions.
