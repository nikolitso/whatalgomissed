Five women meet regularly at a beauty salon in Beirut, where the hair removal is done with caramelised sugar, the "caramel" of the title. Layale, played by Labaki herself, runs the place and is stuck in an affair with a married man. Nisrine is about to marry and is terrified her fiancé's family will discover she isn't a virgin. Rima is drawn to a beautiful customer. Jamale, an actress, refuses to accept she is getting older. Across the street, the seamstress Rose has given up her own life to care for her eccentric older sister.

It is a warm, sensual microcosm where they share forbidden loves, repressed desires, the fear of ageing and the weight of duty. Labaki, who came from music videos, gives it a honeyed look and a gentle rhythm, but there is steel underneath. Every one of these women is negotiating with a society that watches her body more closely than she does.

Most importantly, Labaki's debut pointedly refuses to show Beirut as war-ravaged. The shoot finished just before the 2006 war broke out, which gives its calm, ordinary city a poignancy she couldn't have planned. It premiered in the Directors' Fortnight at Cannes and became the biggest success of Lebanese cinema abroad. Light on its feet, generous to every character, and quietly braver than it looks.
