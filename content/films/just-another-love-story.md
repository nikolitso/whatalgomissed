Jonas is a crime scene photographer in Copenhagen with a comfortable, boring life: a wife, two children, a house in the suburbs. One day his car is involved in an accident with a young woman, Julia, who ends up in hospital in a coma. When she wakes up, she has lost her memory and can barely see. Her family mistakes Jonas for her boyfriend, a man none of them has ever met, and Jonas doesn't correct them.

It starts as a dark romantic comedy about a man who walks into someone else's life and likes it there. Then the real boyfriend's past begins to surface, and Ole Bornedal's neo-noir turns into something considerably darker. Bornedal, who made Nightwatch, loves genre and plays with it openly: flashbacks, a narrator speaking from an unexpected place, sudden violence, and colours that look like a pulp paperback cover.

Anders W. Berthelsen is very good as Jonas, a man who seems mild until you realise how much he wants this lie. Nikolaj Lie Kaas, in a smaller role, brings real menace. Not every twist convinces, and the style sometimes runs ahead of the story. But it is slick, twisty and confident, the kind of well-constructed genre thriller Danish cinema occasionally produces and rarely exports. A great late-night film.
