November 2011. Rome is about to lose its government, and the city's crooks and politicians are racing to close one last deal: a huge development on the waterfront at Ostia that will turn the coast into a Las Vegas on the Tyrrhenian. Three men converge in Rome's underworld: a corrupt politician, a crime boss with old far-right connections, and a small-time criminal who keeps making the wrong call. One night of bad decisions sets everything in motion.

Stefano Sollima, who directed the Romanzo Criminale and Gomorrah TV series, knows this world like few others. Adapted from the novel by Giancarlo De Cataldo and Carlo Bonini, the film is dark, rain-soaked, and propulsive, counting down the days like a ticking bomb. The city itself feels like a character: the ruins and the nightclubs and the Vatican all part of the same rotting organism. Pierfrancesco Favino is superb as the politician, Claudio Amendola is chilling as the quiet boss known as Samurai, and Alessandro Borghi, as the Ostia gangster Number 8, became a star overnight.

The style is pure modern noir: neon, rain, synth music by M83, and violence that arrives without warning. It later became a Netflix series, but the film is tighter and meaner. Sollima went on to Hollywood with Sicario: Day of the Soldado. Italy's best thriller in twenty years.
