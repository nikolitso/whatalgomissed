A young woman is found dead by a mountain lake in the north of Italy, lying on the shore as if asleep. Inspector Sanzio arrives from the city to investigate a place where everyone knows everyone and nobody wants to talk. He has troubles of his own at home, with a wife who is slipping away from him into illness, and he carries them quietly into every interview.

Andrea Molaioli's first feature, adapted from a novel by the Norwegian writer Karin Fossum, is less a whodunit than a portrait of a community and the secrets a small place keeps. Every family Sanzio visits has its own grief, its own shame, its own reasons for lying. The film is about the ways people protect each other from the truth, and the cost of that protection. Molaioli keeps the pace slow and the frame clean, and lets the cold mountain landscape do a lot of the talking.

Toni Servillo is flawless, as always. His Sanzio is dry, patient and faintly ironic, a man who listens more than he speaks and notices everything. Watch how much he does with a pause or a half smile. The film won Best Film at the David di Donatello Awards, along with a string of other prizes, and it made Molaioli's name overnight. An Italian crime film that cares more about people than plot, and is better for it.
