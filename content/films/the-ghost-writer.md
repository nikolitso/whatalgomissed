A professional ghost writer, played by Ewan McGregor and never given a name, is hired to finish the memoirs of Adam Lang, a former British Prime Minister. The previous ghost drowned off the ferry. Lang is holed up in a glass-and-concrete house on a grey island off the New England coast, and within days of the writer's arrival he is accused of handing terror suspects over for torture. The writer starts reading the manuscript closely and begins to suspect that his predecessor's death was no accident.

Polanski adapted Robert Harris's novel with Harris himself, and he directs it at his most controlled. There are no big action scenes. The tension comes from wind, rain, empty beaches, a satnav that remembers a route, a car that won't start. Pierce Brosnan plays Lang as a charmer whose charm is starting to run out, Olivia Williams is superb as his sharp, unhappy wife, and Alexandre Desplat's score keeps everything slightly off balance. It is hard not to read the film against the Blair years, and Polanski clearly enjoys letting you.

He finished the editing while under house arrest in Switzerland, which gives a film about a man trapped in a house an odd extra layer. It won him the Silver Bear for Best Director at Berlin. A perfectly constructed political thriller in the old style, the kind Hollywood hardly makes anymore, that gets under your skin and stays there.
