Bruno is a teacher in Milan, gloomy, numb, keeping his life at arm's length. His sister calls him back to Livorno, the seaside town he fled years ago, because their mother Anna is dying. Anna was the most beautiful woman on the beach in the summer of 1971, crowned at a local contest, and that small moment of glory set off a jealousy that broke the family apart. She has been too much ever since: too loud, too flirtatious, too cheerful in the face of disaster.

Virzì, himself from Livorno, moves between past and present in flashbacks, and the structure is the point. We see the mother through the eyes of a boy who was embarrassed by her and a man who still can't forgive her. It is a film about the complicated love we have for the parents who embarrassed us, the ones who were too much and not enough at the same time. The title comes from Nicola Di Bari's old song, and the film has the same mix of sweetness and ache.

Micaela Ramazzotti is extraordinary as the young Anna, and Stefania Sandrelli plays her in old age with a lightness that hides everything. Valerio Mastandrea, all hunched shoulders and silences, is the perfect son. Ramazzotti and Mastandrea both won at the David di Donatello Awards, as did the screenplay. Virzì at his most tender, and Italian comedy at its most humane.
