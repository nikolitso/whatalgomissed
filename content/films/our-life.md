Claudio is a construction worker on the outskirts of Rome, young, loud and very much in love with his wife Elena. They have two boys and a third child on the way. Then Elena dies giving birth. Claudio tries to hold everything together, his three children, his grief and his sense of himself as a man, by throwing himself into dangerous work and worse decisions: a building contract he can't afford, money borrowed from the wrong people, a secret on the site he decides to bury.

Daniele Luchetti films this world without condescension: the half-built apartment blocks, the immigrant labourers paid in cash, the shopping centres where families spend Sundays. Nobody is a saint and nobody a villain. Claudio's brother and sister help where they can, his neighbours do too, and the money keeps disappearing. The funeral scene, where Claudio sings a Vasco Rossi song to his wife, is one of the most painful and beautiful moments in recent Italian cinema.

Elio Germano won Best Actor at Cannes, sharing it with Javier Bardem, for a performance of sustained physical and emotional rawness. He doesn't ask for sympathy; he just keeps going, making mistakes at full speed. This was his second film with Luchetti after My Brother Is an Only Child, and their best together. The most Italian film about masculinity and grief I have seen.
