Anne is a successful lawyer who represents young victims of abuse. She has a beautiful modern house outside Copenhagen, twin daughters and a loving husband, Peter. Then Peter's teenage son from his first marriage, Gustav, comes to live with them: sullen, troubled, difficult. Anne begins a sexual relationship with him. The film never pretends this is anything but a crime, and it never lets her off the hook.

May el-Toukhy makes the film deliberately, uncomfortably one-sided. We only ever have Anne's perspective, which means we only ever see what she allows herself to see. The camera lingers on bodies and summer light, and that is exactly the trap: for a while the film looks like a seduction, and you realise you have been watching through the eyes of someone who knows precisely what she is doing. The second half, when the consequences arrive, is colder and more frightening than any thriller.

Trine Dyrholm makes you understand, if not forgive, every step of the descent. It is one of her greatest performances, and that is saying something. The film won the Audience Award for world cinema at Sundance and the Nordic Council Film Prize, and it confirmed el-Toukhy as one of the sharpest voices in Danish cinema. One of the most morally uncomfortable Danish films I've watched, and one I could not stop thinking about.
