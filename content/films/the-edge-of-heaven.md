Six lives across Germany and Turkey: a retired widower in Bremen and his son, a university professor; a Turkish woman who works as a prostitute and the political activist daughter she is trying to support; a German student and her mother. They are connected by loss, separated by borders, and they keep missing each other by the smallest margins. A train passes another train. Someone leaves a room just as someone else enters.

Fatih Akin's companion piece to Head-On is quieter and more patient than that furious film, and constructed with the precision of interlocking grief. Akin divides it into chapters whose titles tell you in advance that someone will die, which takes away the suspense and puts something heavier in its place. You watch these people walk towards what you already know, and the question becomes what the survivors will do with it. The film moves between Bremen, Hamburg and Istanbul as if they were neighbourhoods of the same city, which for Akin's characters they almost are.

Hanna Schygulla, Fassbinder's great actress, gives the film its emotional centre as a mother who has to cross a border she never imagined crossing. It won Best Screenplay at Cannes in 2007, and it deserved it: few films about migration are this generous to everyone in them. People keep just failing to find each other. Akin makes you ache for them anyway.
