Sonia is a Slovenian chambermaid working in a Turin hotel. Guido is a former policeman, now a security guard at a country villa, still carrying the grief of a dead wife. They meet at a speed-dating evening, and against the odds something real starts between them. Then, during a weekend at the villa he guards, everything goes very wrong.

That is all you should know going in. Giuseppe Capotondi, making his first feature after years in music videos, builds the film like a trap. It starts as a melancholy love story between two lonely people, slides into noir, and then into something close to the supernatural, where you are no longer sure whose head you are inside. The title refers to those moments when the clock shows the same hour and minute, like 23:23, when you are supposed to make a wish. It fits a film obsessed with coincidence and second chances.

Kseniya Rappoport holds it all together. She plays Sonia with a guarded stillness that makes every scene readable two ways, and she won Best Actress at Venice for it. Filippo Timi is a fine, heavy-hearted match as Guido. Not every turn of the plot survives a second look, but on first viewing it grips hard. Trust nothing you see in the first half.
