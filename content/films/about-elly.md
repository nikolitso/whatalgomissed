A group of old university friends, now married with children, leave Tehran for a weekend by the Caspian Sea. Sepideh, the organiser, has brought along Elly, her daughter's kindergarten teacher, hoping to set her up with Ahmad, recently divorced and back from Germany. At first it is all laughter, games and charades. Then Elly disappears.

What follows is a slow, suffocating unravelling. The friendliness fades, the finger-pointing begins, and one small lie triggers a moral collapse that leaves no one clean. Who was Elly, really? Why did Sepideh bring her? Who knew what? Farhadi constructs his trap with the patience of a watchmaker: every character believable, every accusation plausible, every choice understandable and damning at once. The sea outside is loud and indifferent. Inside the villa, the lies get smaller and more desperate.

The cast is a who's who of Iranian cinema. Golshifteh Farahani is superb as Sepideh, nervous and guilty long before she knows why. Taraneh Alidoosti makes Elly a mystery without ever turning her into a device, and Shahab Hosseini and Peyman Moaadi would return in A Separation two years later. Farhadi won the Silver Bear for Best Director at Berlin, and it was the film that made the world pay attention to him. It is also a sharp look at the pressures on women in Iran, all the sharper for never saying it out loud. His most Hitchcockian film, and arguably his most elegant.
