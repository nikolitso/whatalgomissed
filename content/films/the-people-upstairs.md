Julio and Ana have been together for many years, and it shows. They bicker, they barely touch, and they can't remember when things went quiet. Ana has invited the upstairs neighbours to dinner, the couple whose noisy lovemaking comes through the ceiling every night. Julio is not pleased. Then, over drinks, the neighbours make an unexpected proposal that turns the evening into an emotional earthquake.

Cesc Gay adapts his own stage play, and you can feel the theatre in it: one apartment, four people, one night, almost all of it dialogue. It doesn't matter. Gay, who made the lovely Truman, has a gift for conversation that hurts while it makes you laugh. Spain's answer to Perfect Strangers, it is sharper, funnier, and more honest about long-term relationships than most films dare to be. Each of the four gets a moment where the mask slips, and the laughs get more nervous as the evening goes on.

Javier Cámara is perfect as Julio, grumpy, defensive and quietly terrified, and the rest of the cast keep pace with him. What the film understands about boredom and desire, and the stories couples tell each other to keep going, is uncomfortably precise. It is short, tight and adult, and it will start arguments on the way home. A small film that knows exactly what it is doing, and a good one to watch with your partner, if you dare.
