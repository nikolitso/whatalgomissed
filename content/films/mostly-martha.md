Martha is the head chef of a smart restaurant in Hamburg. She lives entirely for her work, sends dishes back to customers who ask for more salt, and talks about recipes to the therapist her boss makes her see. Then her sister is killed in a car accident, and Martha suddenly has to care for her eight-year-old niece Lina, who refuses to eat. At the same moment a charming Italian chef, Mario, arrives to share her kitchen, singing, improvising, breaking all her rules.

Sandra Nettelbeck uses food as a language: cold and precise in Martha's hands at first, warm and generous in Mario's. Martina Gedeck is wonderful as Martha, rigid and funny without knowing it, and slowly coming apart. Sergio Castellitto brings the right lightness as Mario, and the scenes between him and the little girl are some of the best in the film. Nothing is overplayed. Grief here looks like control, and control looks like competence, until it doesn't.

It is a film that understands the relationship between control and grief without announcing it. It became an international art-house hit and was later remade in Hollywood as No Reservations with Catherine Zeta-Jones, but the original has something the remake lost: a German coolness that makes the warmth, when it finally arrives, feel earned. Quietly irresistible. Don't watch it hungry.
