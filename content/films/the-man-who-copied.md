André works a photocopier in a stationery shop in Porto Alegre. He is nineteen, broke, and spends his evenings watching the girl in the apartment across the street through binoculars. To get close to her, he needs money. So he starts counterfeiting cash on the copy machine, and develops a habit that quickly spins out of control.

Jorge Furtado, best known for his celebrated short Isle of Flowers, starts it as a quirky romantic comedy and then shifts gears into something darker, a crime film with robberies, lies and worse. Somehow he pulls both off simultaneously. The film is packed with ideas: André's voiceover, his comic-book drawings that come alive as animated sequences, fragments of Shakespeare he has read in pieces at the copy shop, and a heist logic that keeps folding back on itself. It shouldn't hold together. It does, because Furtado never loses sight of these young people and their very ordinary longing for a better life.

Lázaro Ramos is charming and quietly sad as André, and Leandra Leal is great as Sílvia, the girl across the street, who turns out to be much more than an object of desire. The film was a big success at home and swept the Brazilian awards. It also has a genuinely surprising ending, which I won't spoil. Barely seen outside Brazil, and one of the most inventive comedies of its decade.
