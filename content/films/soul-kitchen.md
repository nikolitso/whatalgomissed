Zinos is a German-Greek owner of a ramshackle restaurant in a Hamburg warehouse, serving frozen schnitzel to loyal regulars, and trying to hold everything together. There is his slipped disc, his new knife-throwing chef who refuses to cook what the customers want, his girlfriend who has moved to Shanghai, an old schoolmate who wants the building, and his ex-con brother Illias who keeps creating disasters. Nothing goes right for long. That is the fun of it.

Fatih Akin, best known for the fury of Head-On and The Edge of Heaven, is here in his most purely enjoyable mode: warm, funny, overflowing with music and food and Hamburg neighbourhood energy. He wrote it with Adam Bousdoukos, who plays Zinos and once ran a restaurant himself, and you can feel that it comes from real life. Moritz Bleibtreu is very funny as the useless brother, and Birol Ünel steals every scene as the chef. When the place finally turns into a club, with soul records spinning and the whole neighbourhood dancing, the film becomes a party you want to join.

It won the Special Jury Prize at Venice, which surprised people who thought Akin only did tragedy. It isn't deep, and it doesn't pretend to be. It is a film about friends, family and a place that belongs to all of them. Akin at his most generous. Go hungry.
