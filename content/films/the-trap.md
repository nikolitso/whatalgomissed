Mladen is a civil engineer in Belgrade, an honest man with a modest job. His wife Marija teaches, and their young son Nemanja is the centre of their life. Then the boy is diagnosed with a heart condition, and the operation that can save him is abroad and costs far more than they could ever pay. Marija places an advert in the newspaper asking for help. A stranger answers. He will cover the full cost, in return for one thing, and Mladen must decide how far a parent will go.

Golubović builds a neo-noir from moral desperation, set against the rotten landscape of post-Milošević Serbia. The war is over, the old system has collapsed, and in the new one everything has a price. Gangsters and businessmen drive the same cars. Honest people like Mladen are the losers of the transition, and the film shows the humiliation of that without ever making a speech about it. The grey housing blocks and empty streets of Belgrade do the talking.

Nebojša Glogovac is superb as Mladen, a decent man whose face slowly closes as he moves deeper into the trap. You understand every step he takes, and that is the most disturbing part. Golubović keeps the plot tight and the tone cold, and the tension doesn't let go for a moment. A strong reminder of how good the cinema of the former Yugoslavia can be when it looks hard at itself. Completely gripping.
