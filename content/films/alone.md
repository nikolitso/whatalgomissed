Alper is a successful chef in Istanbul, in his mid-thirties, with his own restaurant, a beautiful apartment, a collection of old records, and an isolated life of luxury and one-night stands. He likes it that way, or tells himself he does. Then he meets Ada, a modest designer who runs a small clothes shop, and falls for her properly. Now he must figure out whether he is actually capable of the life that would require.

Çağan Irmak, who had already moved Turkish audiences with My Father and My Son, makes a romance that takes its subject seriously. The question is not whether they will get together. It is whether a man can change what he fundamentally is, and what happens to the woman who believes he can. Cemal Hünal plays Alper with a cool charm that slowly cracks, and Cansu Dere gives Ada warmth and a quiet steel. Istanbul itself, its old streets and record shops and late-night restaurants, is almost a third character.

Irmak isn't afraid of emotion, and some will find the music and the melancholy a little heavy. I didn't. The film knows exactly what it is: a grown-up love story about selfishness, fear of commitment and the price of both. It was a huge hit in Turkey, and you can see why. Romantic without being naive, sad without being cruel. A Turkish film that deserves to be much better known abroad.
