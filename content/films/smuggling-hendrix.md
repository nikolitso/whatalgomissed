Yiannis is a washed-up musician in debt to the wrong people, and he is finally leaving crisis-ridden Cyprus for a better life abroad. Then his dog, Jimi, slips his lead in Nicosia and wanders across the UN buffer zone into the Turkish-occupied north. Getting into the north is easy. Getting a dog back out is not: the rules say animals can't cross from north to south. To rescue him, Yiannis must navigate a bureaucratic and political absurdity that the island has lived with for forty years.

Marios Piperides plays it as a comedy, and it is genuinely funny. Adam Bousdoukos, the restaurant owner of Soul Kitchen, gives Yiannis a hangdog charm that carries the whole film, and the people he meets on the other side of the line, Turkish Cypriots and settlers alike, are drawn with real warmth. Piperides uses a dog and a divided city to say something honest about borders, belonging, and a conflict that has become so normalised that even its absurdity no longer shocks. Every checkpoint gag has a sting in it.

It won the top international narrative prize at Tribeca and Best Screenplay from the Hellenic Film Academy, which is rare recognition for a Cypriot film. It is small and modest, and that is part of its strength: it never lectures, it just lets you see how strange it all is. Funny, warm, and quietly furious. The best Cypriot film I have seen.
