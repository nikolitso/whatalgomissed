Desirè works as a hairdresser on film sets in Rome, the one stable member of a family that is anything but. Her younger brother Claudio is fragile and drifting, and their parents are charming, loud, self-absorbed and reckless, the kind who pull their children in and out of disaster and call it love. When Claudio's state gets worse, Desirè has to decide how far she will go to get him out from under their influence.

This is Micaela Ramazzotti's directorial debut, and she also plays Desirè. She brings to it the same emotional precision she has shown as an actress in films like Like Crazy, where she played a woman on the edge with unforgettable intensity. Here she is the one holding it together, and the strain shows in every scene. The film is often funny, in the way that family chaos is funny from the outside, and then suddenly it isn't.

What it captures best is a specific, damaging kind of love: the parent who is also a child, the parent you have to keep saving, and the guilt that comes with every attempt to break away. Max Tortora and Anna Galiena are brilliantly awful as the parents, never cartoonish, always recognisable. It premiered at Venice in the Horizons Extra section and won the audience award there. A confident debut, warm and painful in equal measure.
