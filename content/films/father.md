Michal is a young father in Slovakia, married to Zuzka, with a little daughter he adores. On an unusually hot summer morning he sets off to drop her at nursery and then goes to work, at a company struggling to stay afloat. Hours later he realises that she never got out of the car. What follows is the life of a man who has to carry an unbearable guilt, a marriage tested to its limit, and the threat of prison.

Tereza Nvotová, who wrote the script with Dušan Budzak from events that happened to his best friend, is less interested in the tragedy itself than in what comes after it. She films in long, unbroken takes and stays very close to her characters, so you live every moment with them, including the quiet ones when no one is watching. Milan Ondrík is extraordinary as Michal, a man trying to understand how his own mind failed him, and Dominika Morávková is just as strong as Zuzka, torn between grief and love for her husband.

It is a film about guilt and memory, about forgiveness, and about whether love can survive the worst thing that can happen to two people. The director has called it a love story rather than a film about a tragedy, and that is how it feels by the end. Harrowing, honest and deeply humane.
