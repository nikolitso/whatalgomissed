Lára is a single mother in Iceland, broke, struggling, living with her young son on the edge of losing their home. She finally gets a job training as a border guard at Keflavík airport. On one of her first shifts she flags the forged passport of Adja, an asylum seeker from Guinea-Bissau trying to reach Canada. It is a small bureaucratic act, the job doing what it is supposed to do. But it becomes something more human and more complicated than either woman planned.

Ísold Uggadóttir's debut is about two people trapped by systems, one by poverty and one by bureaucracy, and the unexpected solidarity that forms between them in the space those systems leave. Neither woman is a saint. Both are just trying to survive. Kristín Þóra Haraldsdóttir and Babetida Sadjo are both excellent, and the film gives them room to be tired, proud and wary of each other. The boy, curious and honest, becomes the bridge between them.

This is not the Iceland of travel posters. Uggadóttir shows the grey edges near the airport, the cold industrial outskirts, the cars that become homes, a wealthy country where people still fall through the cracks. She won the Directing Award at Sundance, and the film swept Iceland's Edda Awards, including Best Film. Restrained, humane, never sentimental. A small Nordic film with a very big heart.
