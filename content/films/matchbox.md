A grumpy middle-aged man in Athens is having a hard time with his business partner and a hell of a time with his family. It sounds like the setup for a domestic comedy, but it isn't. Economides traps you in a sweltering apartment, strips away every comfort, and refuses to let you look away.

Almost all of it happens in the shouting: a family that only communicates at full volume, swearing as punctuation, old grievances thrown back like hot coals. It is funny for ten minutes. Then you realise nobody is joking. The camera stays close, the heat never breaks, and the concrete of an Athenian summer presses in from every window.

This was the film that announced Economides and changed Greek cinema in one move. Years before the "Weird Wave", it showed a Greece without postcards, where the problem isn't the crisis outside but what people do to each other inside four walls. The characters are like matches: too much friction, too little space. One spark is all it takes. Hard to love, impossible to forget.
