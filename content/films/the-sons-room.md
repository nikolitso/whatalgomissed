Giovanni is a psychoanalyst in Ancona, a quiet port town on the Adriatic. He has a good marriage to Paola, two teenage children, Andrea and Irene, and a steady stream of patients whose problems he listens to with patience and a little boredom. One Sunday morning he is called away to a patient instead of going running with his son. Andrea goes diving with friends and doesn't come back.

What follows is not a study in grief so much as a study in the impossibility of grief: the way ordinary life keeps interrupting catastrophe, the way a family holds together and pulls apart at the same time. Giovanni replays that Sunday morning again and again in his head. Paola, played with great tenderness by Laura Morante, cries openly. Irene goes on playing basketball as if nothing has happened. His patients keep coming, and suddenly he can't stand their small complaints.

Moretti made his name with ironic, talky, very personal comedies like Dear Diary, often playing a version of himself. Here he plays it straight, and the result is his most moving film. He refuses every form of sentimentality and every form of resolution. Nobody learns a lesson. There is no healing speech. There is just time, and a letter from a girl the family never knew existed. It won the Palme d'Or at Cannes in 2001, and it has aged beautifully. A small film about the largest thing.
