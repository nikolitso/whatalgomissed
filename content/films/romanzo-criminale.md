The rise and fall of the Banda della Magliana, the criminal gang that controlled Rome's underworld through the 1970s and 80s, and had connections to the secret services, the Vatican and the far right. Michele Placido adapts Giancarlo De Cataldo's novel, itself based on the real gang, and follows three friends who start as street kids and end up running the city: Libanese, the ambitious one, Freddo, the loyal one, and Dandi, the one who likes the money most.

Epic in scale, intimate in its tragedy: three friends bound together by loyalty to something that will eventually consume all of them. Placido moves through a decade and a half of Italian history, from the kidnapping of Aldo Moro to the Bologna station bombing, and shows the gang being used by forces far more powerful and far more cynical than they are. Pierfrancesco Favino, Kim Rossi Stuart and Claudio Santamaria are superb, and Stefano Accorsi plays the policeman who spends years chasing them and slowly loses himself in the process.

Italy's answer to Goodfellas, with more politics and more heartbreak. It is long and occasionally overstuffed, but it never loses its grip. It was later adapted into an excellent TV series by Stefano Sollima, who went on to make Suburra, but the film came first, and it remains the best way in. Essential for anyone who wants to understand how crime and the state shook hands in modern Italy.
