Jacob is a Dane who has spent years running an orphanage in Bombay. He has made the poverty around him his home and has little time for the comfortable country he left behind. The orphanage is running out of money, so when a wealthy Danish businessman, Jørgen, offers a large donation, Jacob reluctantly flies to Copenhagen to meet him. Jørgen invites him to his daughter's wedding that weekend. There, Jacob recognises someone from his past, and everything that follows is constructed from that recognition.

This is Susanne Bier at her most precise, working from a script by Anders Thomas Jensen. The plot is built with clockwork tension from entirely domestic materials: secrets, money, guilt, family loyalty. It keeps revealing new layers until the final scene, and every reveal changes how you see the people in the room. Bier's handheld camera and extreme close-ups, eyes and hands and nervous mouths, make it feel almost unbearably intimate.

The acting is superb. Mads Mikkelsen plays Jacob as a man whose idealism is also a way of hiding, and Rolf Lassgård is a force of nature as Jørgen, generous, overbearing and far more complicated than he first appears. Sidse Babett Knudsen is excellent as his wife. The film was nominated for the Oscar for Best Foreign Language Film, and Bier would win it a few years later with In a Better World. Melodrama, yes, but melodrama made with intelligence and nerve.
