Czechoslovakia, 1983. Anna is a gifted young sprinter with a real shot at the Los Angeles Olympics. She is selected for the national team and placed in a secret state-run medical programme, given injections of anabolic steroids to boost her performance. Her coach calls it support. Her body starts to say otherwise. When she discovers the truth and refuses, her mother secretly continues the injections, disguised as vitamins.

The mother, Irena, is the most interesting character in the film. She was once an athlete herself, she is a dissident who listens to Radio Free Europe at night. For her, Anna's success is not just pride. It could be a way out: a trip to the West, and maybe a chance to defect. Anna Geislerová plays her with a fierce, frightening love, and Judit Bárdos is excellent as the daughter caught between her mother, her coach and the state.

Andrea Sedláčková keeps the period detail precise and grey, and she never lets the film turn into a simple lecture on doping. It is about how a totalitarian system gets inside a family, until even love becomes an instrument of control. And of course history has its own cruel twist in store. A very human portrait of an inhumane regime, and a sports film where the real opponent never appears on the track. The Czech Oscar submission for 2015.
