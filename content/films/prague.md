Christoffer and Maja, a married Danish couple, travel to Prague to collect the body of his father, a man Christoffer had not seen in years. It should be a few days of paperwork and a coffin on a plane home. It isn't. The marriage is already cracking, and the city keeps handing them things they did not come for. The father's phone rings, and a life Christoffer knew nothing about starts to come into view.

Mads Mikkelsen plays Christoffer with a stiffness that slowly gives way, a man who decided long ago not to know his father and now has to live with what that cost. Stine Stengade matches him as a wife who has her own secrets to carry. Ole Christian Madsen shoots Prague in winter greys, hotel rooms and offices, a beautiful city reduced to corridors and bureaucracy. The grief arrives sideways, through small humiliations and misunderstandings in a language neither of them speaks.

What follows is a quiet masterpiece about grief, estrangement, and the things we never got to say to the people we decided not to know. Madsen would go on to make Flame & Citron and Superclasico, but this is the film of his I return to. One of the most underrated Danish films of the decade, almost entirely unknown outside Denmark, and it deserves far better.
