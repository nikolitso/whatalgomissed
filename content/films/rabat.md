Nadir has to drive his father's old taxi from Amsterdam to Rabat, a delivery job he does not want. His two friends invite themselves along, and what starts as a lark becomes a trip none of them expected. They travel through the Netherlands, Belgium, France, Spain and Morocco, picking up a hitchhiker in France, getting arrested in Spain, and slowly uncovering the real reason Nadir agreed to make the trip.

Directed by Victor Ponten with Jim Taihuttu, it is a small film made with real affection. The three friends are Dutch-Moroccan young men who grew up in Amsterdam, and the further south they drive, the less sure they are of who they are. In Holland they are Moroccans. In Morocco they are the boys from Europe. The jokes, the arguments and the music in the car carry the film, and the actors have the easy rhythm of people who have known each other for years.

It is not a perfect film. Some of the episodes along the way are stronger than others, and you can see where the budget ran thin. But the honesty is the point. It is a road movie about identity, friendship, and what it means to belong to two places at once, made by people who clearly know the subject from the inside. Warm, funny and more touching than it has any right to be.
