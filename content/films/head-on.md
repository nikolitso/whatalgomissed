Cahit is forty, an alcoholic in Hamburg who has given up on everything. He drives his car into a wall and wakes up in a psychiatric clinic. There he meets Sibel, a young Turkish-German woman who has tried to kill herself to escape her suffocatingly traditional family. She makes him an offer: marry her, so she can leave home, and she will live her own life without bothering him. A marriage of convenience between two people who are both, in different ways, trying to disappear.

Two lost souls who gradually, against every instinct, fall in love, and then destroy what they've found. Fatih Akin shoots it with punk energy, all bars, fights, cocaine and bad nights, cut with interludes of a Turkish band playing by the Bosphorus like a Greek chorus. The second half moves to Istanbul and changes rhythm completely. Birol Ünel and Sibel Kekilli give performances of real recklessness, as if they were risking something personal in every scene.

It won the Golden Bear at Berlin in 2004, the first German film to do so in many years, and the European Film Award for Best Film. It also gave German cinema a new voice, a director from the Turkish community telling stories nobody else was telling. Raw, chaotic and deeply felt. Fatih Akin's most visceral film, and still his best.
