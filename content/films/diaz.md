Genoa, July 2001. The G8 summit has brought hundreds of thousands of protesters to the city, and a young man, Carlo Giuliani, has already been shot dead by police. On the night of 21 July, police storm the Diaz school, where activists, students and journalists are sleeping. What follows is a beating of people in their sleeping bags, fabricated evidence, and days of abuse at the Bolzaneto detention centre. The European Court of Human Rights later ruled that what happened amounted to torture.

Daniele Vicari reconstructs it from court records and testimony. He follows a handful of characters, including a journalist, an elderly activist, a police officer and some young foreigners, through the hours before the raid, and returns to the same moments from different angles. The approach is methodical and factual. It is not a polemic but a document, and that is exactly why it is so profoundly disturbing. When the raid finally comes, Vicari doesn't cut away.

The cast, including Elio Germano and Claudio Santamaria, play it without heroics. The full title adds "Don't Clean Up This Blood", which says it all. Not easy to watch. Impossible to forget. One of the most important Italian films of the 21st century, and one of the least celebrated, perhaps because so many people would rather it hadn't been made.
