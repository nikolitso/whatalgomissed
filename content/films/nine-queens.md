Juan is a small-time hustler working a petty scam at a petrol station in Buenos Aires when Marcos, an older and much smoother con man, steps in to rescue him. Marcos needs a partner for the day. By lunchtime they have stumbled onto the deal of a lifetime: a sheet of rare stamps, the Nine Queens, a forgery good enough to sell to a wealthy collector who is leaving the country the next morning. All they have to do is close the deal before he goes.

Fabián Bielinsky's debut is one of the great Argentine thrillers: propulsive, twisty and completely confident in its execution. Ricardo Darín is wonderful as Marcos, a man who lies as easily as he breathes, and Gastón Pauls plays Juan as the nervous apprentice you can never quite read. The whole film takes place over roughly one day, and the pace never drops.

Underneath the games there is real bite. It was released just as Argentina was sliding into economic collapse, and the city it shows is full of people trying to cheat each other, from street hustlers to bankers. Everyone is running a con; only the scale changes. Hollywood remade it as Criminal, which nobody remembers. Bielinsky made only one more film, El Aura, before his early death in 2006, which makes this one even more precious. The ending lands like a punch.
