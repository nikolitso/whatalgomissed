Aya is thirty and lives with her parents in Tozeur, in the south of Tunisia, at the edge of the desert. She works as a cleaner in a resort hotel and hands every dinar she earns to her family. Her life is decided for her: by her parents, by the neighbours, by what is expected of a young woman who is not yet married. Then one day an accident happens, and everyone believes Aya is dead. She is not. For the first time in her life, she has a choice. She leaves, takes a new name, and starts again in the city.

Mehdi M. Barsaoui turns what could have been a simple escape story into something much richer. Aya's freedom is real, and it is exhilarating to watch: new clothes, new friends, nights out, a life that finally belongs to her. But a woman without papers, without a past, is also a woman without protection, and when she becomes a witness to something she was never meant to see, the city shows her its harder face. Fatma Sfar is superb as Aya, watchful, funny and fierce, carrying almost every scene.

It is a film about identity and freedom, about what a society takes from women and what it costs them to take it back. The tension builds steadily, and the questions it asks stay with you long after the end. Loosely based on real events, it premiered in the Orizzonti section at Venice. Gripping, intelligent and very moving. A must see.
